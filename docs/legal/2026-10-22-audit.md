## Legal review and release checklist

Additional deletion detail: `RenderedDocumentArtifact` cascades on organization deletion but not on the data-only wipe; `MessagingAccountKey` cascades when its device is deleted. The Meta signed-request deletion callback removes selected local advertising/lead records, but is not a general outbound provider-erasure process. Its status/credential handling warrants separate review (`src/app/api/meta-ads/delete-data/route.ts`).

- [ ] Verify GRL Enterprises legal form, business-address status, required identifiers and existing contact mailbox. Do not invent a named officer. Required grievance/DPO/representative details are release blockers for applicable markets.
- [ ] Identify actual customer jurisdictions and legal roles; obtain counsel review for India, EEA, UK, Singapore and any other markets actually served. Do not infer worldwide compliance.
- [ ] Confirm Indian DPDP commencement against Gazette and any amendments at release. The 2025 rules stage Rule 4 at one year and most substantive rules at eighteen months after publication; they must not all be described as effective before their commencement. Check currently applicable IT/SPDI, consumer, tax and incident-reporting duties separately.
- [ ] Confirm active provider legal entities, contracts, billing tier, training opt-ins, retention, subprocessors, hosting/log/backup regions and onward transfers. Complete register and execute required SCCs/UK instrument/assessments; the DPA is not a transfer instrument.
- [ ] Approve retention periods for each inventory row, legal basis, deletion owner, legal holds, backup overwrite and restore-suppression procedure. No arbitrary time limits inserted.
- [ ] Resolve deletion and credit P0 defects with isolated tests before claiming comprehensive erasure or idempotent billing. Review partial failure, cross-tenant protection, stale synchronization and >250-user workspaces.
- [ ] Confirm commercial billing period, immediate cancellation, refunds for qualifying failures, duplicate charges, credit adjustments, any add-on offer and refund communications. No recurring billing or add-on expiry implemented here.
- [ ] Counsel approve liability cap (including confidentiality/IP/indemnities), mandatory carve-outs, DPA duties, breach procedure, audit arrangements, e-signature limitations and international consumer remedies.
- [ ] Set prospective effective dates only after approval; send appropriate material-change notices and obtain any required renewed acceptance/consent. Store version, timestamp, actor and order/workspace acceptance through a separately reviewed implementation. Do not impose revised obligations retroactively.
- [ ] Verify the approved deployment on irisos.app, all routes and links, signed-out access and mobile/desktop layout. This change does not deploy or publish.

## Internal incident response procedure — proposed for approval

1. Record detection time, affected systems, reporter and restricted evidence; escalate through the existing support contact to a business-appointed incident lead. Assign lead, security, counsel and customer-contact owners before release.
2. Contain affected credentials, sessions, integrations or routes; preserve necessary evidence with access logs. Avoid copying unnecessary personal content into tickets.
3. Determine scope, tenant impact, categories, countries, exploit path, affected processors and controller/processor role. Preserve a timeline, evidence integrity and uncertainty.
4. Remediate and verify isolation, rotate affected secrets, recover cleanly and prevent erased data being restored or re-imported. Do not treat restoring service as closing the incident.
5. Counsel assess applicable legal and contractual clocks from awareness/determination, affected-person risk, and regulator/customer notices. GDPR processor notice is without undue delay; controller 72-hour supervisory notification applies where its statutory conditions are met. Indian CERT-In/other obligations and phased DPDP obligations require separate applicability review. No universal deadline is promised.
6. Notify the appropriate customer/controller, individuals, providers and authorities as required, using verified facts and progressive updates; document reasons for decisions and any delays.
7. Conduct post-incident review, track corrective owners/dates and retention/legal holds; rehearse the procedure before representing it as an established operational control.
